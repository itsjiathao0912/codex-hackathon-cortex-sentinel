# Research scope and evidence rules

Objective: research how Alipay and WeChat Pay handle fraud and anti-money-laundering risk associated with their BNPL/consumer-payment-credit products.

Working scope: mainland China; Huabei (花呗) and Fenfu (分付). Explain adjacent products when needed to avoid confusing payment wallets, credit scoring, consumer loans and pay-later merchant services. Research date: 31 October 2026. A page accessed on this date is not necessarily evidence of a policy newly effective on this date.

## Required outputs

1. Identify the products and distinguish payment operators, lending providers and technology entities.
2. Separate fraud prevention, credit underwriting/default risk, and AML responsibilities.
3. Explain supported controls across onboarding, checkout, merchant monitoring, post-transaction monitoring, review and reporting.
4. Compare the two ecosystems and identify product restrictions that matter to risk.
5. Distinguish product-specific practices, platform-wide disclosures, historical descriptions, regulatory requirements and analytical inference.
6. Provide a dated source register, limitations and open questions. Do not claim access to proprietary models or unpublished thresholds.

## Evidence hierarchy

- A: Official product contracts, privacy policies, current help pages, regulator publications and corporate primary disclosures. Each establishes only what its scope supports.
- B: First-party technical publications or research papers. They establish a described method/system at publication time, not necessarily deployment in a particular BNPL product today.
- C: Credible independent reporting, used for context or corroboration and labeled accordingly.
- D: Search snippets, promotional summaries or unsourced assertions. Discovery only, not sufficient for a substantive conclusion.

## Claim labels

PRODUCT = documented for the named product; PLATFORM = payment/financial ecosystem disclosure; HISTORICAL = dated description whose current deployment is unverified; REQUIREMENT = legal or regulatory obligation, not proof of implementation; INFERENCE = reasoned interpretation; UNKNOWN = insufficient public evidence.

## Guardrails for interpretation

Cash-out, repayment delinquency, account anomalies and suspicious transactions are different concepts. An unusual pattern is a lead, not proof of fraud or money laundering. A regulator-imposed requirement does not establish a provider's operating effectiveness. Privacy notices describe data processing permissions/purposes; they do not prove every listed datum enters every risk model. Do not infer production model thresholds, filing volumes or detection rates without direct evidence.
